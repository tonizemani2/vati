# VOICE.md - research, PDF, essay, and site voice

## Reference target

Use Leopold Aschenbrenner's *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead* (June 2024, https://situational-awareness.ai/) as a structural reference, not as text to copy. The goal is the feeling of "the world is moving, here is the evidence, here is what follows," paired with the discipline of an institutional investor research note.

Think: Situational Awareness-style strategic sweep + BlackRock/JPMorgan-style research apparatus + Vati's own constraint-migration thesis. Not corporate white paper. Not generic AI prose.

## House Voice

- Write like a sharp analyst with live situational awareness: direct, specific, evidence-led, and willing to take a side.
- Make the core thesis obvious early: what is changing, why now, who is mispricing it, what constraint is moving, and what would prove us wrong.
- Prefer concrete nouns, dates, named actors, mechanisms, and quantities over abstract claims.
- Keep the tone intelligent and alive. It can have narrative momentum, but it should never feel theatrical, salesy, or mystical.
- Every important claim should answer: "what data would I look at next Monday to know if this is becoming true?"

## Institutional Research Discipline

- Treat serious PDFs and research outputs as decision memos, not blog posts.
- Include charts, tables, or diagrams wherever they make a claim easier to inspect. Most substantial PDFs should have several real visuals, not one decorative chart.
- Favor visuals that expose a mechanism: trendlines, bottleneck stacks, supply/demand gaps, dependency graphs, scenario tables, timelines, winner/loser matrices, and pricing-vs-reality comparisons.
- Every chart needs a clear title, unit, date window, and source note. If a chart is based on a model or estimate, say so plainly.
- Add confidence, base/upside/downside cases, first observable trigger, and kill condition for forecast-like claims.
- Keep calibration visible: probability, interval, time horizon, and what would update the view.

## What To Avoid

- Do not copy or closely paraphrase the Situational Awareness essay. Store links and style principles, not the essay text.
- Avoid AI-scented filler: "delve," "unlock," "transformative," "landscape," "tapestry," "robust," "seamless," "underscore," "not merely X but Y," and generic "in an era where..." openings.
- Avoid corporate mush: "leverage synergies," "navigate uncertainty," "empower stakeholders," "future-proof your organization."
- Avoid fake balance. If the evidence points one way, say it; then mark the uncertainty and the falsifier.
- Avoid decorative charts. If a visual does not change what the reader understands, cut it.

## Useful Structure

1. The live anomaly: what no longer fits the consensus view.
2. The mechanism: the constraint, dependency graph, supply elasticity, and pricing gate.
3. The evidence: data, trendlines, examples, and counterevidence.
4. The forecast: what happens next, by when, with probability or scenario weights.
5. The watchlist: triggers, kill criteria, and data to monitor.
6. The action: what a buyer, investor, operator, or policymaker should do differently.

## Site And Landing Copy

- The site should feel like a research cockpit and investment-grade intelligence product, not a SaaS brochure.
- Lead with the product signal: dated calls, track record, live research, calibration, and buyer-useful forecasts.
- Use concise copy with real nouns. Avoid explaining the product in vague "AI platform" language.
- When possible, show proof visually: forecast cards, record tables, calibration charts, research previews, and constraint maps.
