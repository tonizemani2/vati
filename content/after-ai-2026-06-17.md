## P1 — Firm power becomes the scarce input in AI

### LinkedIn

By May 2026 the wait for a large grid transformer in the US had stretched to about four years. That one number is quietly changing where the entire AI buildout gets sited.

For two years the limit on frontier AI was compute, and whoever secured the most GPUs set the pace. Chips are still scarce and expensive, but they can be bought. Firm power, delivered to a specific place on a specific date, increasingly cannot. Lawrence Berkeley National Lab expects US data centers to draw 7 to 12 percent of all American electricity by 2028, and utilities cannot build to meet that on the schedule the buildout assumes.

Once power is the gate, the value of a data center comes down to one question: can the site generate its own electricity. A campus running on firm generation behind the meter, outside the utility queue, can energize a year or two ahead of a cheaper site still waiting on a transformer. When idle GPUs and unsigned leases burn cash every month, that head start is worth real money.

The gain accrues to two groups. Developers of geothermal and other always-on clean power, who can turn one data center into a decade-long offtake and skip part of the grid queue. And owners of land that already carries power rights, who can now compete on how fast a tenant gets energized. The exposure runs the other way for campuses stuck in congested grids, where the megawatts in the press release are more real than the megawatts that will flow, and for investors who underwrote shells on the assumption they could be powered on time.

This is a dated forecast, the kind we expect to be graded on. Nothing here resolves until the end of 2028, and we are not claiming a past call. We hold it at 82 percent on the direction and 52 percent on the strict version. The signal to watch is the first major cloud operator or data center landlord announcing a campus above 100 megawatts that names its own clean firm power as the reason to build there. If almost none do that by late 2028, or grid waits ease back under two years, the call was wrong.

**When the AI buildout met a wall it can't buy through**
*For two years the limit was chips. It is becoming firm power, and that resets who wins the next phase.*

**The four-year transformer.** By May 2026, the quoted lead time for a large grid transformer in the US had reached about four years. Order one now, energize around 2030. A hyperscale data center, by contrast, can be built in roughly eighteen months. When the part that connects you to power takes longer than the building, the transformer is the critical path, and the project lives or dies on electricity rather than real estate. Most coverage of the AI buildout still treats this as a footnote. It is the main story.

**Why the grid can't answer.** The demand is not speculative. Lawrence Berkeley National Lab projects US data centers consuming 7 to 12 percent of the country's electricity by 2028. The grid cannot be expanded at that rate. Adding generation, high-voltage equipment, and interconnection approvals runs on timelines measured in years, and the queue for a large new load is already long. The gap between how fast AI operators want to deploy and how fast the power system can absorb them keeps widening. Chips are expensive but available. Firm power, in the right place on the right date, increasingly is not.

**The value moves to the site.** This changes what a data center is worth. For years the variables were land cost and fiber. Now the question that matters is whether a site can generate its own firm power behind the meter, outside the interconnection queue. A campus that can do that energizes a year or two ahead of a cheaper site still waiting on a transformer. In a business where idle GPUs and unsigned leases burn capital every month, that head start is worth more than a lower rent. The market sees that power is tight. What it still underprices is siting treated as an energy-development problem, where next-generation geothermal can bypass part of the grid queue entirely.

**Winners and exposure.** If the call is right, the value accrues to two groups. Developers of geothermal and other always-on clean generation, who can turn a single data center into a decade-long offtake and skip the queue. And owners of land that already carries power rights, who can compete on how fast a tenant gets energized. The exposure runs the other way for grid-dependent campuses in congested markets, where the megawatts in the announcement will not actually flow on schedule, and for investors who underwrote shells on the assumption they could be powered on time.

**The call, and how it dies.** Stated plainly and with a date: by the end of 2028, the scarce input in AI infrastructure is firm power and site rights, the way GPU access was the scarce input in 2024. We hold it at 82 percent on the direction and 52 percent on the strict version of the claim, and the gap between those two numbers is the honest measure of what is still uncertain. The signal that it is happening will be the first hyperscaler or major data center REIT to announce a campus above 100 megawatts and name its own clean firm power as the reason to build there. The call is wrong if almost none appear by late 2028, or if transformer and interconnection waits in the main US markets normalize back under roughly two years. Nothing here has resolved. It is a forecast on the record, and it will be graded.
