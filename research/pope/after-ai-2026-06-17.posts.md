# Desk notes — After AI (and one near-term commodity call)

Source: Pope board `after-ai-2026-06-17` (notes 1-3) and `any-short-2026-06-15` P3 (note 4).
Compiled 2026-06-19.

These are forward calls. None has resolved; the earliest resolution date is 2027. Each note states
a dated, falsifiable view, the odds we hold it at, what would change our mind, and the first public
signal to watch. They describe where value concentrates. They are not investment advice. Probabilities
are shown as direction / strict-clause, the two numbers the board carries.

Each note stands alone and can be posted as written, on LinkedIn or as an X long-form. Re-check any
dated fact in note 4 before it goes out.

---

## 1. The binding constraint in AI moves from compute to power

We expect the scarce input in AI infrastructure to shift from chips to firm, deliverable power by 2028.
The asset that reprices is the site, not the processor.

Data center electricity demand is rising faster than the grid can be built. Lawrence Berkeley National
Lab projects US data centers at 7 to 12 percent of national electricity by 2028. Supply cannot match
that pace. As of May 2026, lead times for large power transformers were close to four years, and grid
interconnection for a major new load often takes longer. Compute is becoming easier to buy than the
power to run it.

This turns data center siting into an energy question. A site that can generate firm power on its own,
behind the meter and outside the interconnection queue, can energize 12 to 24 months ahead of a cheaper
site waiting on the grid. That time advantage is worth more than a lower land cost.

Positioning. Value accrues to developers of geothermal and other firm clean generation, who turn data
center load into a bankable long-term contract, and to owners of land that already holds power rights.
It moves away from grid-dependent campuses in congested markets, where announced capacity overstates
deliverable capacity, and from investors financing shells that cannot be energized on schedule.

Forecast: 82% on the direction, 52% on the strict 2028 formulation. Resolves 2028-12-31.
What would change our view: fewer than two hyperscale-scale campuses securing behind-the-meter firm
clean generation as a stated siting advantage by end-2028, or US grid lead times normalizing below
roughly 24 months.
Watching: the first hyperscaler or major data center REIT to announce a 100 MW-plus campus whose stated
edge is its own clean firm power rather than cheap land.

## 2. In robotics, the value is in deployment, not the robot

We expect the durable margin in physical AI to sit in certified deployment, the work of making a robot
reliable and safe on a real line, rather than in the robot hardware.

The visible part of robotics is the machine. The cost is in everything after the demo: teaching the
robot a specific task, validating it until it is reliable, fitting it into a workcell, certifying it as
safe, and supporting it in the field. Most of this is software and process. A robot that needs six
months of integration to perform one narrow task has weak economics even when the body is cheap.

The larger players are already organizing around this. Nvidia sells not only chips but a simulation and
validation stack, Isaac Sim, Isaac Lab, Cosmos, and GR00T, that sits between training a robot and
trusting it in production. Industry analysts such as IDTechEx identify tactile sensing, training data,
and simulation as the parts that gate deployment, not the chassis.

Positioning. Value concentrates in simulation platforms on the validation path, in integrators who turn
one-off installation into reusable task libraries, and in manufacturers with clean process data and
standard workcells who deploy fastest. It compresses for undifferentiated humanoid makers and for
custom-only integrators whose bespoke labor is undercut by reusable tooling.

Forecast: 78% on the direction, 46% on the strict 2028 formulation. Resolves 2028-12-31.
What would change our view: humanoid and mobile-manipulation deployments scaling mainly through turnkey
hardware, with little separate spend on validation, commissioning, or safety, by end-2028.
Watching: a major automotive or logistics rollout whose own announcement credits the validation,
simulation, or task-library layer for the scale-up.

## 3. For enterprise AI agents, the constraint is control, not capability

We expect spending on agent authority and auditability, the controls that let an agent act on real
systems, to become a separate enterprise budget line by mid-2028.

Agents that answer questions are easy to pilot. Agents that move money, change records, or operate
systems create liability the moment they act. At that point the limiting factor is not model quality
but control. Berkeley's risk profile for agentic AI identifies the failure modes directly: improper
tool access, privilege escalation, and unsupervised tasks that execute incorrectly. A stronger model
does not address them.

The scarce layer is the apparatus that makes an agent trustworthy with real access: an identity for the
agent, least privilege so it reaches only what a task requires, approval steps for sensitive actions,
complete audit logs, reversible execution, and evidence acceptable to a regulator or insurer.

Positioning. The beneficiaries are identity and access vendors, whose permissioning maps directly onto
agent authority; agent platforms that build in logging and rollback and become the control plane for
live systems; and insurers, who can price risk once controls are auditable. Prompt-only agent
frameworks and capability-first model vendors do not clear procurement for regulated action.

Forecast: 74% on the direction, 48% on the strict formulation. Resolves 2028-06-30.
What would change our view: large enterprises deploying agents with real authority on mostly
prompt-level guardrails and generic logging, with no separate action-governance budget, by mid-2028.
Watching: the first Fortune 500 RFP, insurance policy, or regulator to require agent action logs,
approval chains, and rollback before production deployment.

## 4. Nickel: a metal surplus is masking a battery-grade squeeze

We expect the price premium for battery-grade nickel sulfate over LME nickel to widen materially through
mid-2027, even as headline nickel stays in surplus.

Reported nickel surpluses describe the metal traded on the LME, where Indonesia has oversupplied the
lower-grade nickel used in stainless steel. The battery supply chain is tightening at the same time. As
of mid-June 2026, a sulfuric acid shortage in Indonesia is curtailing MHP, the mixed hydroxide
intermediate refined into battery-grade nickel sulfate. A surplus in the metal and a deficit in the
battery-grade chemical can coexist, because high-nickel cathodes have no fast substitute for
sulfate-grade nickel.

The cost lands on high-nickel cell makers and the automakers committed to them, and it widens the cost
gap in favor of LFP chemistry, which uses no nickel sulfate. Producers of Class-1 sulfate outside the
acid-intensive Indonesian route collect the scarcity premium.

Forecast: the sulfate premium over LME 3-month nickel widens past 4,500 dollars per tonne by
2027-06-30. Direction held at 72%, the strict magnitude at 41%.
What would change our view: the premium holding within 2,000 dollars per tonne, or the acid shortage
resolving, for example a durable reopening of the Strait of Hormuz that cuts sulfur costs.
Note: the supply facts above are dated mid-June 2026 and should be re-checked before publication. This
is the nearest-term call on the board.
