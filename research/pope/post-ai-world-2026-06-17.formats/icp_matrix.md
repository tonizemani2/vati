# ICP Matrix - The World After AI: intelligence gets permission to act

Rank segments by painful timing errors, budget ownership, and whether the watch signal changes a real decision.

## 1. P1 - agentic commerce / identity / payments

Buyer roles: Payment networks, issuer processors, merchant acquirers, cloud agent platforms, identity vendors, and enterprise risk teams that expect agents to move money or make commitments.

Expensive decision: Payment and identity roadmap priorities shift from human checkout UX toward agent-specific credentials, programmable limits, merchant trust signals, and liability handling.

Wedge: Early rails win standards gravity. If the agent economy turns into high-volume microtransactions, the infrastructure provider that owns credentialing and settlement gets transaction take-rate and compliance lock-in before apps commoditize.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P1.

## 2. P2 - liability / insurance / audit evidence

Buyer roles: AI assurance firms, cyber insurers, regulated enterprises, autonomous vehicle and robotics operators, healthcare AI vendors, and enterprise legal teams.

Expensive decision: AI compliance budgets shift from one-off model evaluations to persistent logging, forensic replay, evidence retention, and independent assurance.

Wedge: A firm with usable incident evidence can defend claims, obtain coverage, and fix systems faster. A firm without it pays through exclusions, discovery costs, downtime, and regulatory penalties.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P2.

## 3. P3 - autonomous science / lab infrastructure

Buyer roles: National labs, pharma R&D operations, materials companies, analytical-instrument vendors, lab automation vendors, and cloud scientific-workflow platforms.

Expensive decision: R&D infrastructure budgets shift from isolated robot workcells toward interoperable lab operating systems, data schemas, and integration standards.

Wedge: A standards-first lab stack avoids repeated custom integration, lets experiments move across sites, improves reproducibility, and creates a market for experiment capacity.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P3.

## 4. P4 - materials / qualification / reliability

Buyer roles: Battery, aerospace, semiconductor, catalyst, energy equipment, and advanced-manufacturing firms evaluating AI-generated material candidates.

Expensive decision: R&D budgets shift from model-only discovery tools toward reliability testbeds, reference datasets, and standards participation.

Wedge: The cost of waiting is a stranded candidate portfolio: many plausible materials and no credible path to product insertion. Qualification data can shorten design-in cycles and prevent expensive late failures.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P4.

## 5. P5 - biosecurity / synthetic biology / access control

Buyer roles: DNA synthesis providers, biofoundries, benchtop synthesizer makers, universities, pharma, cloud labs, and institutional biosafety officers.

Expensive decision: Biofoundry and synthetic-biology platform budgets shift from raw synthesis speed toward compliance screening, access control, and auditable procurement.

Wedge: Institutions that clear compliant procurement can keep research flowing while others face funding restrictions, halted orders, or reputational damage after a misuse incident.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P5.

## 6. P6 - biology / pharma / causal data

Buyer roles: Pharma strategy teams, AI-biology startups, organoid companies, spatial-omics platforms, single-cell technology vendors, and biotech investors.

Expensive decision: Business-development teams prioritize partnerships and acquisitions around human perturbation atlases rather than model architecture alone.

Wedge: A proprietary causal atlas can reduce wrong-target spend, improve translational confidence, and create licensing leverage even if frontier biology models commoditize.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P6.

## 7. P7 - energy / data centers / grid stability

Buyer roles: Hyperscalers, data-center developers, utilities, battery integrators, power-electronics vendors, grid planners, and infrastructure funds.

Expensive decision: Data-center capex moves from lowest-cost megawatts toward interconnection-ready power architecture and utility-approved operating behavior.

Wedge: A site that can connect quickly and satisfy stability requirements beats a cheaper stranded site. The timing advantage can be worth more than headline power price.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P7.

## 8. P8 - data-center real estate / cooling / water / heat

Buyer roles: Data-center developers, hyperscalers, district-energy utilities, municipal planners, water authorities, and infrastructure real-estate investors.

Expensive decision: Site acquisition and power procurement decisions add heat/water rights as first-order diligence, not late-stage sustainability checks.

Wedge: A campus with permitted cooling and waste-heat offtake can avoid delays, community fights, retrofits, and future water or heat-reuse mandates.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P8.
