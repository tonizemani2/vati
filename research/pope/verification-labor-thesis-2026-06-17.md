## Executive Thesis

Agentic AI kills the cheap junior task ladder faster than it kills work itself. The scarce labor migrates from producing first drafts to proving, checking, governing, insuring, integrating, and credentialing AI-mediated work.

The jobs most exposed are not whole professions in one clean wave. The exposed layer is the routine-production layer: generic junior builders, manual QA, tier-one support, routine coordinators, prompt-only operators, final-output graders, generic homework tutors, and content-packaging roles.

The underpriced labor is the verification layer:

- AI evaluation associates
- model QA and red-team operators
- evidence-pack engineers
- AI insurance evidence specialists
- AI workflow operations owners
- verifier-curriculum engineers
- assessment validators and oral-defense examiners
- IEP, assistive-tech, Medicaid, and special-education compliance navigators

Education should stop selling "learn AI tools" as the main promise. The winning promise is: become the person organizations trust when AI output must be verified, governed, insured, procured, integrated, or credentialed.

## Jobs Most Likely To Shrink

The shrinking jobs are not every job with a laptop. The threatened zone is work where the main value is producing, moving, or grading a low-context artifact.

High-risk work:

- first-draft code implementation
- routine manual QA
- tier-one support triage
- routine customer-service responses
- generic junior analysis and report assembly
- admin coordination and status chasing
- scheduling, intake, routing, and data entry
- prompt-only consulting
- generic homework help
- final-output-only grading
- detector-only academic integrity work
- static course content packaging

This is why "learn to use AI" is too weak. Everyone will learn to use AI. The labor premium goes to people who can prove when AI should be trusted.

## Education Strategy

The new education stack should be artifact-first, not lecture-first. A learner should leave with proof that can survive employer, insurer, regulator, school, or procurement review.

### Pillar 1: AI Verification And Evaluation

Teach eval suites, model QA, red-team labs, adversarial cases, rubric calibration, failure taxonomies, and human review protocols.

Required artifacts:

- eval harness repo
- golden set and adversarial set
- red-team report
- release recommendation
- monitoring dashboard

### Pillar 2: Evidence-Pack Production

Teach system cards, impact assessments, monitoring plans, traceability maps, change notices, remediation logs, procurement matrices, and risk acceptance memos.

Required artifacts:

- AI impact assessment
- high-impact determination memo
- QASP
- vendor evidence request
- data lineage and provenance record
- procurement-ready executive memo

### Pillar 3: AI Risk, Insurance, And Audit Trails

Teach AI inventories, use-case tiers, logs, data lineage, validation records, vendor audit rights, incident reconstruction, and insurance/regulatory mappings.

Required artifacts:

- event-log schema
- authority matrix
- incident reconstruction dossier
- broker-ready evidence pack
- tabletop exercise and after-action report

### Pillar 4: Agentic Workflow Operations

Teach process mapping, CRM/ATS/ERP data models, no-code/API basics, permissions, escalation, data quality, audit logs, rollback plans, and handoff liability.

Required artifacts:

- as-is/to-be workflow map
- automation backlog
- working sandbox automation
- SOP and user guide
- ROI dashboard

### Pillar 5: Assessment Integrity And Human Validation

Teach allowed-use policies, process artifacts, oral defense, supervised performance, portfolio curation, apprenticeship sign-offs, and authenticity checks.

Required artifacts:

- AI-exposure matrix
- oral-defense protocol
- supervised-performance runbook
- process evidence schema
- portfolio audit report

### Pillar 6: Special-Education And Compliance Navigation

Teach IEP review, assistive-tech implementation, evaluation workflow, Medicaid documentation, progress monitoring, service-minute tracking, and parent navigation.

Required artifacts:

- IEP lifecycle map
- AT consideration matrix
- evaluation request tracker
- Medicaid documentation packet
- service-minute reconciliation report
- family-facing plain-language summary

For CS grads, QA workers, support leads, analysts, social science grads, and GRC associates. Promise: prove where AI works, where it fails, and what must be monitored.

2. Federal AI Evidence-Pack Engineering Certificate  
For junior engineers, QA/SRE, acquisition staff, GRC, technical writers, program managers, and analysts. Promise: get AI approved, bought, monitored, and renewed.

For admins, coordinators, business ops, project ops, HR ops, RevOps, healthcare admin, nonprofit ops, and adult learners. Promise: become the workflow owner, not the checklist coordinator.

5. Verifier-Curriculum Engineer Certificate  
For teachers, tutors, instructional designers, QA, support QA, trust-and-safety, technical writers, junior developers, and domain analysts. Promise: design rubrics, test cases, review protocols, and learning scaffolds that make AI output usable.

6. Accountable Validation and Performance Assessment Certificate  
For educators, adjuncts, TAs, industry experts, L&D teams, HR assessors, clinical preceptors, shop-floor supervisors, and field leads. Promise: build proof that survives questioning.

7. Special Education Systems Navigator Certificate  
For tutors, paraprofessionals, general educators, medical billers, care coordinators, bilingual liaisons, CHWs, instructional designers, and edtech support staff. Promise: keep the system from dropping the student.

## Thesis Outline

1. The real shock is ladder loss, not instant joblessness.  
AI removes the low-risk first-draft tasks that trained juniors.

2. The bottleneck migrates from production to proof.  
When output is cheap, proof becomes expensive.

3. The new scarce worker is a verifier.  
The job is to test, evidence, monitor, govern, and explain.

4. Education must stop training for the vanishing first draft.  
Prompting and junior coding are weak unless paired with verification, rubrics, test cases, and employer acceptance criteria.

5. Schools become process-evidence institutions.  
Credentials need observed performance, oral defense, process artifacts, and AI-use disclosure.

6. Middle-skill work reprices around agentic operations.  
Routine coordination declines, but workflow ownership becomes valuable.

7. The education product to build.  
Create stackable pathways in AI verification, evidence-pack engineering, insurance evidence, workflow operations, accountable validation, verifier curricula, and special-ed compliance navigation.

## Bottom Line

Do not tell people simply to learn AI. Teach them to become trusted operators of AI-era evidence.

The durable career question is not "Can you make AI produce something?" The durable career question is: "Can you prove the output is correct, safe, accountable, insurable, purchasable, integrated, and credential-worthy?"
