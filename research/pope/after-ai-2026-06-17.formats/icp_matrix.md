# ICP Matrix - After AI: where the constraint moves when intelligence leaves the screen

Rank segments by painful timing errors, budget ownership, and whether the watch signal changes a real decision.

## 1. P1 - AI infrastructure / energy

Buyer roles: Hyperscaler infrastructure teams, data-center developers, power developers, large AI labs with reserved compute needs, and investors underwriting AI infrastructure.

Expensive decision: Data-center site selection, PPA strategy, power-development partnerships, capex phasing, and portfolio exposure to grid-dependent versus power-secured data-center assets.

Wedge: A site that energizes 12 to 24 months earlier can be worth more than a cheaper site with stranded shells and delayed transformers. The asymmetry is time: idle GPUs and delayed leases burn capital while power-secured campuses monetize demand.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P1.

## 2. P2 - robotics / industrial automation

Buyer roles: Manufacturing COOs, logistics operators, industrial automation integrators, robot OEMs, and investors in physical AI companies.

Expensive decision: Automation capex, integrator selection, pilot design, safety certification budgets, and whether to invest in robot OEMs or deployment-layer tooling.

Wedge: A robot that takes six months of integration labor to deliver a narrow task has poor ROI even if the body is cheap. Deployment tooling pays by converting demos into billable productive hours faster.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P2.

## 3. P3 - autonomous science / biotech / materials

Buyer roles: Pharma R&D leaders, materials companies, national labs, AI-for-science founders, and investors underwriting discovery platforms.

Expensive decision: R&D budget allocation between foundation models, lab automation, assay capacity, data infrastructure, and strategic partnerships.

Wedge: A better model has low ROI if every validation loop takes months. Shortening the experiment loop compounds across the entire discovery funnel and creates proprietary datasets competitors cannot scrape.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P3.

## 4. P4 - edge AI / consumer hardware

Buyer roles: Device OEMs, chip vendors, mobile OS teams, consumer AI app developers, regulated enterprise buyers, and privacy/security teams.

Expensive decision: Hardware roadmap, NPU vendor selection, app architecture, privacy posture, and whether to build for cloud-first chat or local context surfaces.

Wedge: Always-on local context can raise retention and trust, while cloud-only interfaces compete on commoditized model access. Battery and privacy are the cost of admission.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P4.

## 5. P5 - industrial biotech / biomanufacturing

Buyer roles: Industrial biotech founders, chemicals strategics, food and materials companies, climate-tech investors, and CDMOs.

Expensive decision: Investment diligence, partnership timing, pilot plant capex, CDMO contracting, and whether to buy design IP or process know-how.

Wedge: Early access to pilot and downstream capacity can turn designs into qualified products while competitors wait in the scale-up queue. Avoiding failed scale-up saves years of burn.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P5.

## 6. P6 - enterprise AI / security / governance

Buyer roles: CISOs, CIOs, compliance leaders, insurers, enterprise software buyers, agent platform vendors, and legal teams.

Expensive decision: Enterprise AI procurement, security architecture, insurance underwriting, compliance budgets, and whether agents are allowed to touch systems of record.

Wedge: A controlled agent can be deployed into high-value workflows that an uncontrolled agent cannot enter. The upside is adoption; the avoided loss is incident cost, regulatory exposure, and procurement blockage.

Best opening artifact: buyer action sheet + watchlist row for P6.
