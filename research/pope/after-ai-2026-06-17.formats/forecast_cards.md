# Forecast Cards - After AI: where the constraint moves when intelligence leaves the screen

## P1: The AI frontier moves from model access to firm-power siting.

Odds: 82% / 52%
Resolves: 2028-12-31
Needle: Contiguous land, fiber proximity, behind-the-meter firm generation rights, interconnection optionality, cooling, and local permitting bundled into a deployable data-center campus.
Buyer: Hyperscaler infrastructure teams, data-center developers, power developers, large AI labs with reserved compute needs, and investors underwriting AI infrastructure.
Action: Map sites by firm-power time-to-energize, not just land cost and fiber; secure options on campuses where geothermal, gas with carbon capture, nuclear restart, or other firm power.
Trigger: A hyperscaler or top data-center REIT announcing a 100 MW plus campus whose stated differentiator is behind-the-meter clean firm power rather than cheap land.

## P2: Physical AI's bottleneck is certified deployment, not robot bodies.

Odds: 78% / 46%
Resolves: 2028-12-31
Needle: Verified task data, sim-to-real validation, workcell commissioning, safety certification, and field support for physical AI in factories and warehouses.
Buyer: Manufacturing COOs, logistics operators, industrial automation integrators, robot OEMs, and investors in physical AI companies.
Action: Inventory repeatable tasks by commissioning burden and safety risk; structure pilots around validated productive hours and time-to-commission, not robot count or demo quality.
Trigger: A major automotive or logistics deployment where the press release names the validation, simulation, or task-library layer as the reason the rollout scaled.

## P3: Autonomous science shifts the bottleneck from model discovery to experimental throughput.

Odds: 76% / 44%
Resolves: 2029-12-31
Needle: Robotic experimental throughput with standardized metadata, reliable assays, calibrated uncertainty, and provenance that lets models learn from results without poisoning the loop.
Buyer: Pharma R&D leaders, materials companies, national labs, AI-for-science founders, and investors underwriting discovery platforms.
Action: Audit which discovery programs are experiment-limited; secure access to robotic testbeds, assay vendors, and metadata/provenance infrastructure before model-generated proposal.
Trigger: A major pharma, chemicals, or materials company publicly saying its AI discovery bottleneck is lab throughput or assay availability, not model quality.

## P4: The consumer AI interface moves to always-on edge devices, gated by thermals, battery, sensors, and privacy.

Odds: 70% / 43%
Resolves: 2028-12-31
Needle: Low-power NPUs, sensor fusion, local memory, privacy-preserving orchestration, and thermal envelopes for always-on personal AI.
Buyer: Device OEMs, chip vendors, mobile OS teams, consumer AI app developers, regulated enterprise buyers, and privacy/security teams.
Action: Design around local context and power budgets; build features that improve when personal data stays on device instead of depending on generic cloud memory.
Trigger: A Google, Samsung, Apple, or Meta product launch where the lead AI feature runs persistently on device and is marketed around private context rather than a bigger cloud model.

## P5: Biomanufacturing's bottleneck is scale-up, not AI organism design.

Odds: 72% / 41%
Resolves: 2030-12-31
Needle: Pilot and commercial-scale fermentation, downstream processing, strain robustness, feedstock flexibility, QA systems, and process-development talent.
Buyer: Industrial biotech founders, chemicals strategics, food and materials companies, climate-tech investors, and CDMOs.
Action: Underwrite AI-bio companies on scale-up evidence, COGS path, downstream process, feedstock contracts, and pilot capacity access before valuing design claims.
Trigger: A well-funded AI-bio or synbio company delaying launch because pilot capacity, downstream recovery, or COGS fails despite successful lab design.

## P6: Agentic AI's scarce layer becomes authority, auditability, and rollback.

Odds: 74% / 48%
Resolves: 2028-06-30
Needle: Identity, permissioning, tool-access control, audit trails, reversible execution, human authorization policies, and liability evidence for agent actions.
Buyer: CISOs, CIOs, compliance leaders, insurers, enterprise software buyers, agent platform vendors, and legal teams.
Action: Design agent deployments around authority boundaries first: least privilege, approval ladders, audit logs, rollback, and evidence packets for regulated workflows.
Trigger: A Fortune 500 RFP, insurance policy, or regulator explicitly requiring agent action logs, approval chains, and rollback for production deployment.
