## Recommended First Wedge

Start with AI infrastructure / energy.

Why this wedge: A site that energizes 12 to 24 months earlier can be worth more than a cheaper site with stranded shells and delayed transformers. The asymmetry is time: idle GPUs and delayed leases burn capital while power-secured campuses monetize demand.

First buyer action: Map sites by firm-power time-to-energize, not just land cost and fiber; secure options on campuses where geothermal, gas with carbon capture, nuclear restart, or other firm power can be contracted behind the meter.

First watch signal: A hyperscaler or top data-center REIT announcing a 100 MW plus campus whose stated differentiator is behind-the-meter clean firm power rather than cheap land.

## Per-Call Action Queue

### P1 - The AI frontier moves from model access to firm-power siting.

Owner: AI infrastructure and power desk

1. Verify: A hyperscaler or top data-center REIT announcing a 100 MW plus campus whose stated differentiator is behind-the-meter clean firm power rather than cheap land.
2. Decide: Data-center site selection, PPA strategy, power-development partnerships, capex phasing, and portfolio exposure to grid-dependent versus power-secured data-center assets.
3. Act: Map sites by firm-power time-to-energize, not just land cost and fiber; secure options on campuses where geothermal, gas with carbon capture, nuclear restart, or other firm power can be contracted behind the meter.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by end 2028 fewer than two hyperscaler-scale campuses publicly secure behind-the-meter firm clean generation as a core siting advantage, or if transformer and interconnection delays normalize below roughly 24 months in the main US AI data-center markets.

### P2 - Physical AI's bottleneck is certified deployment, not robot bodies.

Owner: physical AI and automation desk

1. Verify: A major automotive or logistics deployment where the press release names the validation, simulation, or task-library layer as the reason the rollout scaled.
2. Decide: Automation capex, integrator selection, pilot design, safety certification budgets, and whether to invest in robot OEMs or deployment-layer tooling.
3. Act: Inventory repeatable tasks by commissioning burden and safety risk; structure pilots around validated productive hours and time-to-commission, not robot count or demo quality.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by end 2028 humanoid or mobile manipulation deployments scale mainly through turnkey robot hardware with little separate pricing for task validation, commissioning software, or safety case tooling.

### P3 - Autonomous science shifts the bottleneck from model discovery to experimental throughput.

Owner: R&D and autonomous science desk

1. Verify: A major pharma, chemicals, or materials company publicly saying its AI discovery bottleneck is lab throughput or assay availability, not model quality.
2. Decide: R&D budget allocation between foundation models, lab automation, assay capacity, data infrastructure, and strategic partnerships.
3. Act: Audit which discovery programs are experiment-limited; secure access to robotic testbeds, assay vendors, and metadata/provenance infrastructure before model-generated proposal volume spikes.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by 2029 model-only AI discovery companies repeatedly produce commercially validated materials or therapies without materially expanding wet-lab or physical-test throughput.

### P4 - The consumer AI interface moves to always-on edge devices, gated by thermals, battery, sensors, and privacy.

Owner: edge device and platform desk

1. Verify: A Google, Samsung, Apple, or Meta product launch where the lead AI feature runs persistently on device and is marketed around private context rather than a bigger cloud model.
2. Decide: Hardware roadmap, NPU vendor selection, app architecture, privacy posture, and whether to build for cloud-first chat or local context surfaces.
3. Act: Design around local context and power budgets; build features that improve when personal data stays on device instead of depending on generic cloud memory.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by end 2028 the dominant consumer AI usage remains cloud-chat inside phones and browsers, with wearables and glasses failing to show persistent local context as a major usage mode.

### P5 - Biomanufacturing's bottleneck is scale-up, not AI organism design.

Owner: industrial biotech desk

1. Verify: A well-funded AI-bio or synbio company delaying launch because pilot capacity, downstream recovery, or COGS fails despite successful lab design.
2. Decide: Investment diligence, partnership timing, pilot plant capex, CDMO contracting, and whether to buy design IP or process know-how.
3. Act: Underwrite AI-bio companies on scale-up evidence, COGS path, downstream process, feedstock contracts, and pilot capacity access before valuing design claims.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by 2030 multiple AI-designed industrial bio-products reach commodity-relevant scale and price parity without scarce pilot capacity, downstream processing, or process-development labor becoming a public bottleneck.

### P6 - Agentic AI's scarce layer becomes authority, auditability, and rollback.

Owner: agent security and governance desk

1. Verify: A Fortune 500 RFP, insurance policy, or regulator explicitly requiring agent action logs, approval chains, and rollback for production deployment.
2. Decide: Enterprise AI procurement, security architecture, insurance underwriting, compliance budgets, and whether agents are allowed to touch systems of record.
3. Act: Design agent deployments around authority boundaries first: least privilege, approval ladders, audit logs, rollback, and evidence packets for regulated workflows.
4. Package: sell a 30-day monitor if the decision is live.
5. Stop: Kill if by mid-2028 large enterprises widely deploy multi-step agents with real system authority using mostly prompt-level guardrails and generic logging, without a separate action-governance budget.
