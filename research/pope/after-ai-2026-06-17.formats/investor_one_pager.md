# Investor One-Pager - After AI: where the constraint moves when intelligence leaves the screen

## Claim

Vati is not selling generic prediction. It identifies binding constraints before they change capex, procurement, site selection, compliance budgets, or portfolio exposure.

## Why Now

The shared shift is that AI stops being mainly a model market and becomes a physical deployment market. The obvious layers, GPUs, frontier labs, generic agents, and benchmark wins, are no longer where the edge lives. The edge moves into the inputs that cannot answer a software-speed demand curve: firm power and siting, robot commissioning and safety validation, experimental throughput, always-on edge thermals, biomanufacturing scale-up, and auditable authority for agents that can act. The commercial wedge for Vati is to identify these binding constraints before they change capex, procurement, site selection, portfolio exposure, compliance budgets, or partnership timing.

## Proof Surface

- Public forecasting benchmarks and sealed forward records are credibility surfaces.
- Pope boards are buyer-facing decision artifacts.
- Each call carries a dated clause, kill condition, and practical action.

## Top Calls

### P1 - The AI frontier moves from model access to firm-power siting.

- Odds: 82% structural / 52% exact dated clause.
- Buyer: Hyperscaler infrastructure teams, data-center developers, power developers, large AI labs with reserved compute needs, and investors underwriting AI infrastructure.
- Decision changed: Data-center site selection, PPA strategy, power-development partnerships, capex phasing, and portfolio exposure to grid-dependent versus power-secured data-center assets.
- ROI logic: A site that energizes 12 to 24 months earlier can be worth more than a cheaper site with stranded shells and delayed transformers. The asymmetry is time: idle GPUs and delayed leases burn capital while power-secured campuses monetize demand.

### P2 - Physical AI's bottleneck is certified deployment, not robot bodies.

- Odds: 78% structural / 46% exact dated clause.
- Buyer: Manufacturing COOs, logistics operators, industrial automation integrators, robot OEMs, and investors in physical AI companies.
- Decision changed: Automation capex, integrator selection, pilot design, safety certification budgets, and whether to invest in robot OEMs or deployment-layer tooling.
- ROI logic: A robot that takes six months of integration labor to deliver a narrow task has poor ROI even if the body is cheap. Deployment tooling pays by converting demos into billable productive hours faster.

### P3 - Autonomous science shifts the bottleneck from model discovery to experimental throughput.

- Odds: 76% structural / 44% exact dated clause.
- Buyer: Pharma R&D leaders, materials companies, national labs, AI-for-science founders, and investors underwriting discovery platforms.
- Decision changed: R&D budget allocation between foundation models, lab automation, assay capacity, data infrastructure, and strategic partnerships.
- ROI logic: A better model has low ROI if every validation loop takes months. Shortening the experiment loop compounds across the entire discovery funnel and creates proprietary datasets competitors cannot scrape.

## Monetization Wedge

Start with high-stakes buyers whose decisions are lumpy and timing-sensitive: infrastructure investors, strategy teams, procurement desks, operators, insurers, and funds exposed to physical bottlenecks.
