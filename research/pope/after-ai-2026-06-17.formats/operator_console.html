After AI: where the constraint moves when intelligence leaves the screen

The shared shift is that AI stops being mainly a model market and becomes a physical deployment market.

P1 · AI infrastructure / energy · 52%

The AI frontier moves from model access to firm-power siting.

Now: Map sites by firm-power time-to-energize, not just land cost and fiber; secure options on campuses where geothermal, gas with carbon capture, nuclear restart, or other firm power can be contracted behind the meter.

Buyer: Hyperscaler infrastructure teams, data-center developers, power developers, large AI labs with reserved compute needs, and investors underwriting AI infrastructure.

Watch: A hyperscaler or top data-center REIT announcing a 100 MW plus campus whose stated differentiator is behind-the-meter clean firm power rather than cheap land.

Stop: Kill if by end 2028 fewer than two hyperscaler-scale campuses publicly secure behind-the-meter firm clean generation as a core siting advantage, or if transformer and interconnection delays normalize below roughly 24 months in the main US AI data-center markets.

P2 · robotics / industrial automation · 46%

Physical AI's bottleneck is certified deployment, not robot bodies.

Now: Inventory repeatable tasks by commissioning burden and safety risk; structure pilots around validated productive hours and time-to-commission, not robot count or demo quality.

Buyer: Manufacturing COOs, logistics operators, industrial automation integrators, robot OEMs, and investors in physical AI companies.

Watch: A major automotive or logistics deployment where the press release names the validation, simulation, or task-library layer as the reason the rollout scaled.

Stop: Kill if by end 2028 humanoid or mobile manipulation deployments scale mainly through turnkey robot hardware with little separate pricing for task validation, commissioning software, or safety case tooling.

P3 · autonomous science / biotech / materials · 44%

Autonomous science shifts the bottleneck from model discovery to experimental throughput.

Now: Audit which discovery programs are experiment-limited; secure access to robotic testbeds, assay vendors, and metadata/provenance infrastructure before model-generated proposal volume spikes.

Buyer: Pharma R&D leaders, materials companies, national labs, AI-for-science founders, and investors underwriting discovery platforms.

Watch: A major pharma, chemicals, or materials company publicly saying its AI discovery bottleneck is lab throughput or assay availability, not model quality.

Stop: Kill if by 2029 model-only AI discovery companies repeatedly produce commercially validated materials or therapies without materially expanding wet-lab or physical-test throughput.

P4 · edge AI / consumer hardware · 43%

The consumer AI interface moves to always-on edge devices, gated by thermals, battery, sensors, and privacy.

Now: Design around local context and power budgets; build features that improve when personal data stays on device instead of depending on generic cloud memory.

Buyer: Device OEMs, chip vendors, mobile OS teams, consumer AI app developers, regulated enterprise buyers, and privacy/security teams.

Watch: A Google, Samsung, Apple, or Meta product launch where the lead AI feature runs persistently on device and is marketed around private context rather than a bigger cloud model.

Stop: Kill if by end 2028 the dominant consumer AI usage remains cloud-chat inside phones and browsers, with wearables and glasses failing to show persistent local context as a major usage mode.

P5 · industrial biotech / biomanufacturing · 41%

Biomanufacturing's bottleneck is scale-up, not AI organism design.

Now: Underwrite AI-bio companies on scale-up evidence, COGS path, downstream process, feedstock contracts, and pilot capacity access before valuing design claims.

Buyer: Industrial biotech founders, chemicals strategics, food and materials companies, climate-tech investors, and CDMOs.

Watch: A well-funded AI-bio or synbio company delaying launch because pilot capacity, downstream recovery, or COGS fails despite successful lab design.

Stop: Kill if by 2030 multiple AI-designed industrial bio-products reach commodity-relevant scale and price parity without scarce pilot capacity, downstream processing, or process-development labor becoming a public bottleneck.

P6 · enterprise AI / security / governance · 48%

Agentic AI's scarce layer becomes authority, auditability, and rollback.

Now: Design agent deployments around authority boundaries first: least privilege, approval ladders, audit logs, rollback, and evidence packets for regulated workflows.

Buyer: CISOs, CIOs, compliance leaders, insurers, enterprise software buyers, agent platform vendors, and legal teams.

Watch: A Fortune 500 RFP, insurance policy, or regulator explicitly requiring agent action logs, approval chains, and rollback for production deployment.

Stop: Kill if by mid-2028 large enterprises widely deploy multi-step agents with real system authority using mostly prompt-level guardrails and generic logging, without a separate action-governance budget.
