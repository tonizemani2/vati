## Qualification Questions

Ask these before quoting. The goal is to find a live decision, not to perform free consulting.

1. Which named campus, asset list, market, docket, borrower, seller, or counterparty are you deciding on?
2. What decision has to be made in the next 3 to 12 months?
3. Who owns the budget for diligence, underwriting, approval, site selection, or power procurement?
4. What would change if the claimed MW, interconnection path, PPA, permit, or co-location status is weaker than advertised?
5. What is the current cost of a one-month delay in your own model: lost revenue, carry cost, lease penalty, missed committee date, public backlash, or staff time?
6. What evidence do you already have: RTO queue ID, FERC docket, utility filing, PPA summary, permit number, site-control document, public announcement, or buyer data room?
7. Which label would matter most: `source_verified`, `contested`, `demoted`, or `unresolved_interconnection_risk`?
8. Who receives the final memo, and what action can they take with it?
9. Are there any confidentiality limits? If yes, can the sprint still rely on public records and non-confidential buyer-provided materials?
10. What would make this sprint a clear no-go after day 2?

Disqualify if answers stay category-level: "AI power is important", "we are watching the space", "send us a market map", or "we might invest someday."

## ROI And Risk Logic

Vati should not pretend to calculate a buyer's project economics without buyer inputs. Use a sensitivity frame and force the buyer to supply the live number.

Core ROI equation:

`value = avoided_wrong_commitment + avoided_delay_cost + improved_negotiating_terms + faster_decision - sprint_cost`

The sprint is worth buying if it changes one of these decisions:

- Avoid: do not underwrite, lend to, approve, market, or reserve a campus whose public power claim does not survive the record.
- Reprice: lower valuation, require contingencies, change covenant/draw schedule, change seller reps, or demand milestones.
- Sequence: move the buyer's attention to assets with faster time-to-energize.
- Negotiate: require proof of interconnection, permit, utility agreement, PPA/offtake, equipment order, water/cooling approval, or cost-allocation treatment.
- Monitor: create an escalation threshold tied to a docket, queue, permit, regulatory order, or public project milestone.

Cost of waiting, buyer-specific:

- Infrastructure investor: missed bid window or capital committed to an asset whose energization date slips.
- Lender: term sheet or credit approval written around capacity that later becomes contested.
- Municipality: public incentive or infrastructure commitment made before MW and utility impacts are verified.
- Landowner/developer: sales narrative loses credibility when sophisticated buyers discover gaps first.
- Power developer: origination resources spent on an offtaker/site whose approvals cannot support the claimed schedule.
- Strategic AI buyer: reservation, prepay, or compute plan assumes third-party capacity that cannot energize.

First economic proof target: make the USD 12k-18k sprint pay for itself if it prevents one wrong outside counsel assignment, one bad committee memo, one unsupported public approval, one wasted site pursuit, or one premature non-binding commitment. Do not require Vati to prove a full project return on the first sale.

## Deliverables

The standard sprint should deliver:

1. Verification ledger: every named project, source URL, retrieved date, source tier, evidence span, extracted fields, open question, and label.
2. Time-to-energize risk table: MW/capacity, counterparty/offtake, grid/interconnection/behind-meter path, permit/regulatory status, transformer/switchgear dependency, cooling/water issue, local consent issue, and next date.
3. Refutation/demotion table: at least one tempting lead that does not survive the hard label rule.
4. Action memo: what to reserve, avoid, diligence further, negotiate, monitor, or kill.
5. Budget-owner summary: 1-page committee-ready version with the live decision, decision changed, cost of waiting, and stop rule.
6. 30-day watchlist: owner, cadence, public source, threshold, escalation rule, and kill/stop condition.
7. Appendix of public routes: official dockets, contact forms, procurement routes, IR pages, and agency pages used for next-click follow-up.

Optional paid add-ons:

- Docket monitoring for FERC/PJM/PUC/RTO/permit updates.
- Buyer asset intake template.
- Portfolio exposure screen.
- Anonymous benchmark rows across markets.
- Source-verified data feed for internal tools.

## Scope Boundaries

Vati does:

- Public-source and buyer-provided non-confidential diligence.
- Primary-source extraction from official company, agency, docket, RTO, utility, and permit records.
- Labeling and demotion/refutation of campus power claims.
- Decision memos that state what action the evidence supports.
- Watchlists and stop rules.

Vati does not:

- Invent contacts, emails, named people, permits, projects, prices, or private facts.
- Provide legal, engineering, tax, securities, or investment advice.
- Lobby regulators or manage ex parte communications.
- Certify grid deliverability, engineering feasibility, or credit quality.
- Claim a site is investable solely from a press release.
- Scrape private systems, bypass access controls, or use confidential data without permission.
- Sell a stock pick or a generic AI-power market report as the product.

## Why This Is Not Generic Consulting

Generic consulting sells expert interpretation. This offer sells a repeatable verification object tied to a falsifiable forecast.

Differentiators:

- It starts from a dated, scored thesis with a kill condition.
- It has a hard label rule that can demote the customer's preferred story.
- It preserves source URLs, retrieved dates, source tier, evidence spans, and open questions.
- It requires at least one refuted or demoted lead.
- It is designed to change a named decision, not produce a strategy deck.
- It can become a recurring evidence map and monitoring feed.
- Its credibility comes from being willing to say "not verified", "contested", or "stop".

The product is not Vati's opinion about AI power. The product is the buyer's ability to decide whether a specific campus power claim survives the public record.

## Discovery Call Script

Target length: 25 minutes.

Opening:

"We are not selling the idea that data-center power is scarce. Everyone knows that now. The question we answer is narrower: does this specific AI campus power claim survive primary-source diligence before you underwrite, approve, lend against, reserve, or partner around it?"

Minute 1-5, qualify the decision:

- "What named asset, site list, docket, borrower, counterparty, or market are you deciding on?"
- "What is the decision date?"
- "Who owns the budget or committee outcome?"
- "What happens if the power path is delayed, contested, or missing from the public record?"

Minute 5-10, identify the claim:

- "What has been claimed: MW, offtake, PPA, interconnection, behind-the-meter generation, utility buildout, permit status, water/cooling, or local approval?"
- "Which part do you trust least?"
- "Which part would change the decision if it were demoted?"

Minute 10-15, explain the method:

"We build a source ledger. Each row gets a label: source-verified, contested, demoted, unresolved interconnection risk, or power-secured only if the hard evidence exists. We use public agency, RTO, utility, permit, company, and docket records. One row is deliberately a refutation so the work does not become confirmation theater."

Minute 15-20, frame ROI:

- "What is the cost of a one-month delay in your model?"
- "What is the cost of one wrong committee memo or premature term sheet?"
- "If we show a claim is not yet power-secured, what can you actually do: reprice, condition, delay, walk, negotiate, or monitor?"

Minute 20-25, close or disqualify:

"If you can provide a named asset list and a decision owner, the right next step is a 10-business-day sprint. The deliverable is a verification ledger, action memo, one demoted/refuted row, and a 30-day watchlist. If you do not have a live decision, the free memo is enough for now."

Close question:

"Which live site, PPA, interconnection, approval, financing, or capex decision could this change?"

## Stop Rules

Segment stop:

- Stop a segment after three consecutive qualified calls produce no active asset, budget owner, or timing decision.
- Stop P1 as the lead wedge if five qualified calls produce zero buyer-supplied assets.
- Demote P1 behind the agent-authority/P6 lane if two of three serious buyers say internal teams or incumbents already solve this well enough.

Row stop:

- Stop a dossier row if named MW/capacity, counterparty/offtake, power path, or permit/regulatory status cannot be verified after two focused public-source passes.
- Do not upgrade a row because the story sounds plausible. Plausibility is not source verification.

Deal stop:

- Do not begin paid work without a named decision owner.
- Do not continue past day 2 if the buyer cannot supply the asset identifiers needed for the sprint.
- Do not accept work that requires legal advice, engineering certification, securities advice, lobbying, private contact guessing, or private data access outside permissioned materials.

Product stop:

- If buyers repeatedly ask only for a generic AI power market report, keep that as free content and do not build custom consulting around it.
- If the evidence ledger is not reusable across buyers, the product is becoming bespoke consulting; narrow the scope back to structured source verification.
