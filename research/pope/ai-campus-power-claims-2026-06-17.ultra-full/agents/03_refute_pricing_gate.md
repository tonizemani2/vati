# Agent 3 - Adversarial Refute + Pricing / Consensus Gate

Date: 2026-06-17

Run mode: Codex-native adversarial pass. No deploy. No site edits.

Target thesis: AI campus power-claim diligence, derived from `after-ai-2026-06-17` P1.

## Bottom Line

The broad thesis is not pre-consensus anymore. "AI/data-center growth is constrained by power, interconnection, equipment, local consent, and utility cost allocation" is now openly discussed by DOE/LBNL, FERC, PJM, utilities, hyperscalers, Blackstone/Brookfield/KKR-style infrastructure capital, CBRE/JLL-style site-selection firms, and power advisory shops.

Do not sell "we discovered power-secured AI campuses." That is obvious now.

The still-plausible wedge is narrower: independent, primary-source verification of named campus power claims before a lender, investor, municipality, landowner, smaller developer, or strategic buyer underwrites them. The monetizable object is not a forecast memo. It is a refutation-grade evidence ledger that says: this claimed MW / counterparty / power path / docket / permit / time-to-energize row is verified, contested, demoted, or killed.

If the buyer is a hyperscaler, top data-center REIT, top infra fund, or major utility, assume they already know the broad answer and probably have better private data. Vaticinus only wins if it is faster, more adversarial, cheaper than a major diligence team, or useful to buyers exposed to someone else's claim.

## What Remains Non-Obvious And Monetizable

### 1. Press-release MW is not diligence-grade MW

Many public claims mix nameplate capacity, future framework capacity, PPA volume, grid-connected power, co-located load, behind-the-meter generation, utility service, and aspirational campus scale. Buyers need the chain separated. The sellable unit is a row-by-row verification ledger:

- claimed MW
- source of MW
- counterparty/offtake
- grid, co-located, BTM, PPA, utility-service, or framework path
- FERC/RTO/PUC/local-permit status
- transformer/switchgear/cooling/water/local-consent dependency
- unresolved cost-allocation or transmission-service risk
- next public check

### 2. The negative row is the product proof

The product should always include one demoted/refuted lead. Talen/Amazon Susquehanna is a model case: commercially meaningful, source-rich, and still not clean proof of a fully power-secured campus because the regulatory treatment changed the headline story. A buyer should believe Vaticinus because it kills attractive claims.

### 3. Public-source opacity creates a gap below hyperscaler tier

WRI notes that concrete information on expected data-center energy use and interconnection requests is often hard to access and may be proprietary. That means a primary-source public ledger cannot fully replace private diligence, but it can still be valuable to exposed outsiders: municipalities, landowners, lenders, smaller funds, family offices, regional developers, and strategics checking someone else's assertion.

Source:

- WRI on data-center demand forecast opacity: https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-centers-electricity-demand

### 4. Cross-jurisdiction normalization is annoying enough to pay for

The wedge improves if the deliverable normalizes records across FERC eLibrary, PJM queue/load forecast materials, PUC dockets, local planning agendas, utility tariffs, NRC restart/licensing materials, company filings, and developer claims. Incumbents can do this. The opening is a faster, adversarial, narrowly scoped sprint.

### 5. The buyer value is timing risk, not insight

The paid question is not "will AI need power?" It is:

- Can this named asset energize before my underwriting deadline?
- Which claim in the seller/developer deck is unsupported?
- What condition should go into the term sheet, loan covenant, land option, municipal approval, or PPA?
- Which public docket should trigger a reprice, pause, or walk-away?

## Strongest Objections

1. Incumbent objection: CBRE/JLL/GE Vernova/ICF/WoodMac/law firms/utility consultants already cover site selection, power procurement, interconnection, rate design, and power-market risk.

2. Internal-team objection: Hyperscalers, major data-center developers, large infra funds, utilities, and generation owners already employ energy procurement, interconnection, regulatory, project-finance, real-estate, tax, and local-permitting teams. They may have proprietary queue/utility data Vaticinus cannot see.

3. Data-access objection: Public sources may be insufficient for the decisive fact. The true answer may sit in confidential utility service agreements, queue studies, side letters, EPC schedules, interconnection deposits, transformer allocation, tenant LOIs, or undisclosed local negotiations.

4. Consulting-commodity objection: A 10-day diligence sprint can sell once, but without a proprietary dataset or workflow, it looks like boutique consulting.

5. Liability objection: Saying a site is "verified" can create professional-liability expectations. The vocabulary must avoid implying legal, engineering, investment, or grid-reliability certification.

6. Sales-cycle objection: Infrastructure investors and utilities buy through trust, references, and procurement. A cold sprint offer may need warm introductions and proof artifacts before it converts.

7. Buyer-budget objection: Municipalities and landowners may feel the pain but lack budget; hyperscalers and funds have budget but may not need a small outside provider.

8. Timeliness objection: Dockets and project status move quickly. A static memo decays. The product needs watch cadence and explicit expiry dates.

## Kill Conditions For The Product Wedge

Kill or demote P1 as the lead wedge if any of these occur:

1. Five qualified discovery calls produce zero buyer-supplied assets, dockets, site lists, PPAs, term sheets, or live decisions.

2. Two of the first three serious buyers say their internal team or existing advisors already produce the same diligence at sufficient quality and speed.

3. Three buyer calls in a row agree the evidence is interesting but cannot name a decision it changes within 3-12 months.

4. No buyer accepts a paid design-partner sprint at USD 12k-18k after 20 high-fit manual outreaches plus 5 warm-path asks.

5. The team cannot produce a sample dossier where at least three rows have public primary/official source chains and at least one attractive lead is demoted.

6. The decisive fields are repeatedly unavailable from public sources: MW, counterparty/offtake, power path, interconnection/utility-service status, permit/regulatory status, and next check.

7. A major incumbent releases a free or low-cost, regularly updated public data-center power-claim tracker with docket links, status labels, and watch signals.

8. Qualified buyers insist that only stamped engineering, legal, or power-market opinions are valuable, and Vaticinus cannot partner with a licensed/advisory shop.

9. Gross margin does not improve after two sprints because every dossier is bespoke and manual.

10. The product cannot state what it is not: not legal advice, not engineering certification, not investment advice, not a guarantee of interconnection or energization.

## Revised Status Vocabulary

Use labels that preserve uncertainty. Avoid `power_secured` except in rare cases where the public source chain is unusually complete.

| label | meaning | minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `press_lead` | Mentioned in media, marketing, or rumor; not decision-grade. | URL only; no full field chain. |
| `official_claim` | Company, utility, agency, or project owner makes the claim. | Official source naming project and at least one key field. |
| `source_verified` | Official source verifies the claim but not yet the underlying docket/permit/queue. | Official source plus extracted field. |
| `primary_docket_verified` | Primary regulatory/RTO/PUC/local record verifies a key field. | Docket/order/queue/permit row with date and status. |
| `counterparty_verified` | Offtake, PPA, tenant, utility, or generation counterparty is named in an official source. | Official source naming both sides and deal type. |
| `mw_claim_verified` | Capacity figure is verified, but not necessarily energizable. | MW tied to source, date, and scope. |
| `power_path_verified` | Public record identifies grid service, co-location, BTM, PPA, utility-service, or framework path. | Source says how power reaches or matches load. |
| `time_to_power_plausible` | Evidence chain supports a reasonable energization window, with open dependencies named. | MW, path, counterparty, regulatory status, and next dependency. |
| `contested` | Commercial claim exists, but docket/protest/order/appeal/compliance filing disputes it. | Primary dispute source. |
| `unresolved_interconnection_risk` | Project has a power story, but queue/ISA/service status is incomplete or pending. | Missing/pending interconnection source. |
| `unresolved_cost_allocation_risk` | Ratepayer, transmission, network-upgrade, or tariff cost allocation could change economics. | PUC/FERC/PJM/utility proceeding or protest. |
| `permit_local_consent_risk` | Local zoning, water, air, noise, tax, or community approval remains live. | Local agenda, permit, moratorium, or opposition record. |
| `demoted_missing_link` | One or more decisive fields are missing after focused source passes. | Missing-field note and next attempted source. |
| `monitor_only` | Interesting but not actionable without buyer-specific exposure. | Watch signal and cadence only. |
| `refuted` | Public evidence contradicts the marketed claim or makes it unusable for the buyer decision. | Contradictory primary/official source. |
| `expired` | Evidence is too stale for diligence use. | Retrieval date beyond chosen expiry window. |

## What A Buyer Would Already Have Internally

### Hyperscalers / large AI infrastructure strategics

Likely already have:

- energy procurement and structured PPA teams
- utility relations and regulatory affairs
- interconnection / transmission planning specialists
- data-center site-selection teams with GIS, land, fiber, water, tax, and power layers
- local counsel and permitting consultants
- project-finance and tax-credit expertise
- equipment procurement visibility for transformers, switchgear, generators, UPS, cooling
- direct utility and ISO/RTO conversations
- internal load forecasts and confidential demand/tenant data

What they may still buy: adversarial second opinion, competitor/project monitoring, public-record watchlist, or a red-team dossier for board/investment committee use.

### Major data-center developers / REITs / colocation operators

Likely already have:

- powered-land pipeline and option books
- utility-service request history
- interconnection counsel and external power advisors
- customer LOI / preleasing knowledge
- local economic-development contacts
- construction/EPC schedules and equipment allocation

What they may still buy: third-party credibility for investor/customer conversations, market-by-market watch signals, or refutation of competitor claims.

### Infrastructure funds / lenders

Likely already have:

- technical due diligence providers
- power-market consultants
- project-finance lawyers
- internal investment committee frameworks
- sponsor-provided data rooms

Gaps Vaticinus can attack:

- sponsor claims may be accepted too quickly when outside the fund's core power-market geography
- diligence teams may not normalize FERC/RTO/PUC/local permitting into one buyer-readable status ledger
- smaller funds/family offices may lack dedicated data-center power expertise
- lenders may want a fast red flag before spending on full diligence

### Municipalities / landowners / regional economic-development teams

Likely already have:

- developer presentations
- local planning/zoning records
- utility liaison
- tax-benefit models

Gaps Vaticinus can attack:

- weak ability to verify developer MW/time-to-power claims
- limited view into FERC/PJM/PUC implications
- asymmetric information versus sophisticated developers
- need for simple action labels: approve, condition, delay, request evidence, or reject

### Smaller developers / strategics

Likely already have:

- land/control thesis
- local utility conversations
- site-selection consultants
- partial power procurement support

Gaps Vaticinus can attack:

- lack of independent credibility with investors/customers
- weak cross-market comparison
- no standing docket monitor
- confusion between `official_claim`, `primary_docket_verified`, and `time_to_power_plausible`

## Pricing Gate

Do not price as a generic "forecast" or "AI power report." Price as a bounded diligence sprint tied to a buyer-provided decision.

Recommended first offer:

- USD 12k-18k design-partner sprint, 50% upfront.
- Scope: one market or one buyer-provided asset list.
- Duration: 10 business days.
- Required buyer input: named assets, dockets, seller claims, PPA/interconnection claims, site list, or live underwriting question.
- Deliverables: evidence ledger, demoted/refuted lead, time-to-power risk table, action memo, and 30-day watchlist.

Move to USD 25k-40k only after one anonymized sample dossier and one buyer quote/reference.

Do not accept work where the buyer only wants "what is happening with AI power?" That becomes unpaid education and incumbent-consulting comparison. Accept work only when the buyer can answer: "Which asset, docket, counterparty, site, PPA, loan, permit, land option, or approval decision could this change?"

## Recommendation

Status for P1 commercial wedge: `conditional_design_partner`, not `pre_consensus_core`.

The thesis survives only after being narrowed. The original strategic call, "AI frontier moves from model access to firm-power siting," is structurally true but consensus-heavy. The sellable wedge is the adversarial verification layer: prove which named AI campus power claims survive public primary-source diligence, and make the negative findings legible enough for a buyer to change a term sheet, option, loan, approval condition, or monitoring trigger.

If the next agent wave builds product, build the ledger/status system and one public sample dossier. If the next wave sells, sell against live buyer assets only. If buyers do not bring assets, the wedge is probably just obvious consulting.
